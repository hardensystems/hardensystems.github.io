If I could give my 20-year-old self one piece of money advice, I’d tell him to start investing earlier. I spent a lot of my twenties chasing the job title or salary that would make me feel like I’d made it. Meanwhile, I bought an expensive flat-screen TV and financed a motorcycle that cost me about $300 a month.

## What changed for me in 2017

I was putting about 4% into my 401(k) through my employer’s default contribution. Then I saw a video that used $100 a month over 40 years to illustrate how compounding could work. The ending balance depends on what the investments actually earn. What stuck with me was how much time I could give even a modest contribution to grow.

In 2017, I got intentional about it and started investing 15% of my income. That was the number I chose for myself. The bigger change was deciding what I wanted my money to do before a new purchase or monthly payment decided for me.

## What I’d tell myself at 20

I’d tell myself to spend less time waiting for a salary that made me feel successful and more time building the habit while I had time on my side. I still would’ve worked hard. I just wish I’d started putting some of that money to work earlier.
